TL;DR: FreqGS improves monocular novel view synthesis by combining frequency-guided anchor initialization with error-voxel-based densification, producing sharper details and more complete street-scene reconstructions while improving robustness under lateral viewpoint extrapolation.


FreqGS improves high-frequency detail reconstruction and produces more consistent renderings at extrapolated viewpoints in forward-moving monocular street scenes. On held-out views (top), our method better preserves lane markings and subtle road details, yielding improved regional metric performance. At extrapolated viewpoints (bottom), it preserves more coherent lane geometry and sharper road–sidewalk boundaries, whereas existing methods produce blurred, duplicated, or distorted structures.

Abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables real-time novel view synthesis with high visual quality. However, in forward-moving urban street scenes, existing methods often struggle to reconstruct fine structures and high-frequency details because sparse SfM initialization and local densification heuristics result in incomplete coverage and over-smoothed renderings. To address these limitations, we propose a novel method that jointly leverages region-aware frequency-guided initialization and error-voxel-based densification for high-fidelity monocular novel view synthesis. Our initialization performs frequency-guided pixel sampling within partitioned regions, improving fine-detail representation and coverage in low-texture areas. During training, our densification aggregates multi-view image-space color and frequency discrepancies into 3D error voxels, guiding the selective insertion of new anchors in persistently under-reconstructed regions. We further introduce a frequency-derived direction map as an auxiliary constraint to encourage structural consistency. Experiments on publicly available street-scene datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing 3DGS-based approaches, achieving superior rendering fidelity and fine-detail preservation on held-out views while generating more structurally consistent and visually accurate renderings under viewpoint extrapolation.

Method

FreqGS takes a forward-moving monocular image sequence as input and reconstructs the scene using adaptive neural Gaussian anchors. It first initializes anchors with semantic, depth, and frequency guidance, then optimizes them using color, depth, and frequency-aware losses. During training, image-space errors are back-projected into a 3D error volume to insert new anchors in poorly reconstructed regions, improving fine-detail reconstruction.

Extrapolated View Comparison

Qualitative comparison under increasing viewpoint extrapolation. Rendering results are shown for the original test view and for mild, moderate, and strong lateral camera extrapolation.


Scene 1

As the camera moves farther from the training trajectory, the baseline methods increasingly produce blurred, duplicated, curved, or discontinuous lane markings and road boundaries. Our method preserves a more continuous lane boundary and maintains comparatively stable road structure in the rendered images under mild and moderate extrapolation, while showing fewer distortions under strong extrapolation. Nevertheless, all methods degrade under large viewpoint shifts because the extrapolated regions are only weakly constrained by the monocular training views. Please zoom in on the figure for a clearer visualization of the results.



Scene 2

The dashed boxes indicate selected sidewalk and road-boundary regions, and the corresponding enlarged views highlight local geometric distortions. As the camera moves farther from the observed trajectory, the baseline methods increasingly produce blurred or irregular curb and sidewalk structures. Our method preserves more continuous road boundaries and more coherent sidewalk geometry under mild and moderate extrapolation, while showing comparatively fewer distortions under strong extrapolation. However, all methods degrade under large viewpoint shifts because these regions are weakly constrained by the monocular training trajectory. Our method maintains more stable road geometry, lane markings, and scene structure over a wider range of viewpoints, while the baselines exhibit earlier blurring, duplication, and geometric distortion.
